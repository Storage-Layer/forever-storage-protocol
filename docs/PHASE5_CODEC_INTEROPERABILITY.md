## 1. Resolution of the Mathematical Discrepancy

During the Phase 5 baseline audit, a nomenclatural discrepancy was flagged:
- Some Phase 4.4 documentation casually referred to "Vandermonde submatrices".
- However, the actual code implementation in [`ReedSolomonCodec`](file:///home/rade/Project/Jobs/fsp/src/coding/reed_solomon.rs) systematically constructs a **Systematic Cauchy Generator Matrix**.

### Why Cauchy is Superior to Vandermonde in $\text{GF}(2^8)$
In small Galois fields like $\text{GF}(2^8)$, standard Vandermonde matrices $V_{i,j} = \alpha_i^j$ frequently fail the Maximum Distance Separable (MDS) property. Specifically, certain $k \times k$ submatrices formed by selecting arbitrary row subsets have zero determinants ($\det(V_{\text{sub}}) = 0$), rendering those specific erasure patterns unrecoverable.

In contrast, a **Cauchy Matrix** over $\text{GF}(2^8)$ is mathematically proven to be strictly MDS:
$$C_{p, j} = \frac{1}{x_p \oplus y_j} \pmod{P(x)}$$
where $x_p = p$ ($p \in 0..m-1$) and $y_j = m + j$ ($j \in 0..k-1$), using field reduction polynomial $P(x) = x^8 + x^4 + x^3 + x^2 + 1$ (`0x11D`).

Because sets $\{x_p\}$ and $\{y_j\}$ are strictly disjoint ($x_p < m \le y_j$), the denominator $x_p \oplus y_j$ is **never zero**, and **every square submatrix of $C$ has a non-zero determinant**.

## 2. Generator Matrix Construction in FSP

The systematic generator matrix $G \in \text{GF}(2^8)^{n \times k}$ ($20 \times 12$) is constructed as:

$$G = \begin{bmatrix} I_k \\ \hline C \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 
1 & 0 & 0 & \dots & 0 \\
0 & 1 & 0 & \dots & 0 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
0 & 0 & 0 & \dots & 1 \\
\hline
C_{0,0} & C_{0,1} & C_{0,2} & \dots & C_{0,11} \\
C_{1,0} & C_{1,1} & C_{1,2} & \dots & C_{1,11} \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
C_{7,0} & C_{7,1} & C_{7,2} & \dots & C_{7,11}
\end{bmatrix}$$

- **Systematic Rows ($0..11$):** Identity matrix $I_{12}$. Data shards are stored in plaintext byte-for-byte without transformation.
- **Parity Rows ($12..19$):** Cauchy matrix $C \in \text{GF}(2^8)^{8 \times 12}$. Parity shards are calculated via linear combinations.
